✦   The Story   ✦

Born into the Landscape

✦

I grew up in a small townland in east Clare, a few fields away from a holy well that was still being visited by the older people in the village. That well started it all.

I studied Folklore and Ethnology at University College Dublin, but the real education happened in the years after — walking the boreens of Clare and Galway, sitting with old women who knew stories their grandmothers had told them, learning that landscape is not background. It is a living archive.

I began guiding in my late twenties, initially reluctantly. I didn't want to be a tour guide. I wanted to be a folklorist. Eventually I understood that the best way to share what I knew was to bring people directly into contact with the places and the traditions — not to lecture, but to facilitate an encounter.

My tours are small because genuine encounter requires intimacy. You cannot have a meaningful relationship with a place from the back of a coach. You have to slow down. You have to stand still long enough to feel the ground beneath your feet.

I am not selling scenery. Ireland has plenty of that and it doesn't need my help. I am offering something harder to find: context. The stories beneath the stones. The names of the things you're looking at. The reason why that hill matters and that water heals and that tree has been spoken to for seven hundred years.

If that sounds like what you need, come and find me.

✦   The Practice   ✦

A Philosophy of Place

☽
🪨

Slowness Is the Method

Most tours move too fast. Tourists accumulate sites the way they accumulate photographs — as evidence of presence rather than the presence itself. My tours visit fewer places and stay longer at each one. The land needs time to register.

📖

Myth Is Not Metaphor

I approach Irish mythology not as charming folklore but as genuine knowledge — of astronomical events, of social organisation, of psychological terrain. When I tell you that Newgrange was the home of the Dagda, I am not being poetic. I am giving you a map.

🌙

The Land Has Agency

I plan every journey carefully, and then I hold the plan lightly. Sometimes the most important thing that happens on a tour is unscheduled. A conversation at a crossroads. A seal watching us from the water. The weather closing in and opening something unexpected. I stay available.

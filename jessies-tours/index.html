✦   Thin Places   ✦

Where the Veil is Thinnest

☽

The Celts called them caol áit — thin places — where the boundary between worlds becomes permeable. Ireland holds more of these than almost anywhere on earth.

🗿

Poulnabrone Dolmen

A portal tomb standing in the Burren since 3800 BCE. The ancestors who sleep beneath it were still remembered when the druids walked this land.

⭕

Drombeg Stone Circle

Seventeen standing stones aligned to the winter solstice sunset. The fulacht fiadh beside them was used for feasting — and perhaps for something older.

💧

Holy Wells

Ireland has over three thousand sacred wells. Each has its own spirit, its own protocol, its own medicine. We visit those that still hum with presence.

🏔️

Croagh Patrick

The holy mountain of Ireland. Pilgrims have climbed this quartzite peak for over five thousand years — long before the saint gave it his name.

✦   Your Guide   ✦

Jessie O'Brien

✦

Born in Clare, trained in folklore at UCD, drawn back to the land by something older than either.

Jessie has spent two decades walking the margins of Ireland — the bogs and cliff-edges, the overgrown ringforts and forgotten holy wells — studying the living mythology of a landscape that most tourists only photograph.

Her tours are not sightseeing. They are a practice: of attention, of presence, of learning to read a land that is still telling its stories to those who stop long enough to hear.
